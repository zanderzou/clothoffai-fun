Limit the color change to the visible garment. A global color adjustment can also tint the face, hands, hair or wall, even when your intended edit concerns only the dress.

This is a fully clothed fashion-editing workflow using an original fictional image or an adult photo you are entitled to edit. It does not require removing clothing or inventing a body beneath it.

## Preserve the garment before adjusting it

Keep an untouched original and work in an editable document. For an original wrap-dress concept, identify the neckline, waist fold, hem and sleeve edges you want to preserve.

The target is the garment's appearance. A color mock-up does not prove the physical fabric exists in that shade or will print identically.

## Apply the adjustment to a selection

Adobe's Photoshop guide describes selecting the object, then adding a Hue/Saturation adjustment so the selection becomes its mask. Its adjustment-layer guidance keeps the correction separate from the original image data.

Select the dress carefully using the available selection tools, then create the appropriate adjustment layer. Change hue and saturation while inspecting the visible result; do not assume an exact slider value works for every source color.

Refine the mask where needed. The provider documents painting black or white on the mask to control the affected area. Check which area is currently affected before applying a large brush stroke.

## Inspect boundaries and texture

Zoom in around fingers crossing the dress, loose hair, sleeve openings and the hem. Look for skin accidentally included in the mask or small garment areas left in the old color.

Toggle the adjustment to compare with the original. Keep folds and seams readable rather than flattening the fabric into a uniform colored patch.

Save the editable working document separately from a delivery copy. This guide describes the documented method and review criteria; it does not claim an actual edit or guarantee that a mask will automatically preserve every detail. The useful outcome is a controlled, reversible color change whose scope you can inspect.
