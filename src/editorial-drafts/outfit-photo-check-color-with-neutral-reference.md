Keep a known neutral gray or white reference close to the garment and under the same light. A warm lamp can give both the background and the garment a yellow cast. Compare them as separate points before adding a color effect.

Adobe’s Camera Raw guide describes using a known neutral object to guide white balance. Photoshop also documents separate masked Hue/Saturation adjustments. Either workflow deserves a visual check of the particular garment.

The two sample cards show why the known neutral matters. A warm decorative swatch can make a poor color reference.

## Fix the light question first

Compare the original with a duplicate balanced to the actual neutral reference. Inspect a bright fold and a shadow fold on the garment. If the reference was under another light, move it beside the garment and retake the photo. The duplicate can provide a reference for review under the same display conditions.

## Change garment color on a separate copy

When you deliberately want to recolor it, preserve the true reference and work on a separate version. Follow seams, buttons and edges while checking where the edit reached. If the picture is meant to represent an item for sale, verify the physical garment under consistent light. Color in an edited image remains a display decision.
