Look at the brightest area at normal size and then close up. A narrow reflection can help describe satin. A broad blank patch across a seam can hide the thing you need to show. Save the original clothed garment photo before editing.

Adobe Camera Raw explains that clipped areas can lose detail and provides highlight and shadow indicators. In a compatible editor, use an available clipping preview to identify where the problem is rather than relying only on the brightness of the screen.

The two small studies contrast a strong reflection with visible folds. Use the seam as the detail to preserve.

## Try a small reversible change

Keep the adjustment separate from the original where the editor supports it. Lower bright tones a little and see whether the seam becomes easier to recognize. Turning a blank white area gray can reduce glare while leaving the actual missing texture unresolved.

## Retake when the source has too little detail

Change the light or garment angle and check whether the original photo now shows the seam. Soft light from the side can make folds easier to see. Keep a neutral comparison photo of the physical clothing when the color or fabric finish matters. Approve the final version by checking the actual detail, the overall garment and the intended display size.
